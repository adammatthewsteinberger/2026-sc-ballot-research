## Greenville County - Travelers Rest 1

**Research snapshot:** September 20, 2026

Prepared from the provided sample ballot and publicly available official records, candidate materials, and independent reporting. This report is informational and does not endorse any candidate.

<!-- PDF page 1 -->

## Scope and ballot verification

The provided PDF is a five-page Greenville County sample ballot for the November 3, 2026 statewide general election. It contains 39 named candidates across statewide, federal, judicial, and county offices. The ballot also contains three local yes/no questions; those questions are not analyzed here because the request concerned candidates.

The ballot's own warnings - that it is a sample, may differ from the official ballot, and should be checked close to the election - are instructions contained in the document, not additional instructions from the requester. The official Greenville County candidate list, Greenville County voter-registration page, and South Carolina Votes candidate-information page were used to verify the slate.

One minor discrepancy appears in the county candidate list: it spells the District 4 incumbent "Williams Timmons," while the ballot and official House biography use "William Timmons." This appears to be a clerical typo, not a separate candidate.

## Research method

The evidence hierarchy was:

- Official election, agency, legislative, court, and government records for offices, service, appointments, duties, and documented events.
- Independent reporting for controversies, political context, and scrutiny.
- Candidate and party websites for positions and biographical claims, explicitly treated as self-reported.

An information gap means that sufficient reliable public information was not located. It is not proof of dishonesty, incompetence, or lack of qualifications. Party affiliation, endorsements, name recognition, and campaign spending were not treated as substitutes for evidence.

The offices also require different standards. Executive candidates should be judged on management and budgets; legislators on votes and coalition-building; prosecutors on charging, diversion, and due process; judges on court administration; and county or conservation officials on measurable local performance.

## Governor and lieutenant governor

These candidates run as tickets. The governor controls appointments, executive agencies, and budget proposals, but many major promises require legislative approval and appropriations.

### Michael Addison and Candace Brewer - United Citizens

Addison's campaign describes him as a medical director who has treated thousands of South Carolinians and as someone with environmental-inspection experience. The United Citizens Party lists Addison and Brewer as its statewide ticket. See Addison's campaign and the party candidate page.

Brewer's independent public profile is limited. Her campaign site primarily presents her as Addison's running mate rather than documenting a substantial public-sector or elected record.

**Critical assessment:** this ticket has limited independently verifiable governing experience and relatively little publicly available detail about budgets, agency management, education, infrastructure, public safety, or health-care implementation. That is an information limitation, not proof of poor qualifications. Voters should ask for a detailed first-year budget, agency leadership plan, and evidence supporting the ticket's health and economic claims.

<!-- PDF page 2 -->

### Walid Hakim and Arnold E. Karr - Green

Hakim has one of the more detailed alternative-party platforms. His proposals include Medicaid expansion, a state-run insurance structure with fixed premiums and no deductibles or denials, public investment in education, social workers responding to some nonviolent calls, and a more interventionist economic and foreign-policy posture. See his campaign platform.

The main weakness is feasibility detail. A state-owned insurance program would raise difficult actuarial, federal-waiver, provider-payment, regulatory, and budget questions. The platform does not appear to provide independent cost estimates, implementation timelines, or legislative language. Karr's public biography and policy material are particularly sparse.

**Critical assessment:** Hakim has expressed more policy substance than many minor-party candidates, but the major proposals are not yet financially or legally demonstrated. Karr remains difficult to evaluate independently because little reliable public information was located beyond the ticket listing.

### Alan Wilson and Mike Reichenbach - Republican

Wilson is the current South Carolina attorney general and has held that office since 2011. Independent reporting identifies him as a four-term attorney general and former South Carolina Army National Guard officer. His campaign emphasizes education, economic development, government efficiency, and a state-level version of "DOGE." See Wilson's campaign and Associated Press reporting.

Reichenbach is a state senator and businessman. His platform emphasizes conservative social policy, including abortion restrictions and support for law enforcement. See his campaign platform.

**Critical assessment:** Wilson has the strongest directly relevant executive experience in this race, which permits voters to examine an actual record rather than only promises. Long tenure also creates accountability questions: What measurable outcomes did his office achieve, and what precisely would change as governor? "Cut waste" proposals should be accompanied by a list of programs, projected savings, implementation costs, and effects on services. Reichenbach's legislative record may help evaluate him, but his statewide executive-management agenda is less developed publicly.

### Jermaine Johnson and Sam Skardon - Democratic

Johnson is a state representative. His campaign says he passed legislation involving senior tax relief, infrastructure, broadband, and rural health, though those claims should be checked against legislative records. His platform proposes an education audit, road and bridge investment, rural hospitals, mental-health response teams, paid family-leave incentives, technical-education investment, and a goal of increasing in-state production of food, energy, and water. See Johnson's policy platform.

Skardon is a former Charleston County Democratic Party chair, clean-energy executive, and former congressional aide, according to South Carolina Public Radio. He does not have a comparable elected or statewide executive record.

**Critical assessment:** Johnson offers a relatively detailed platform, but many proposals - especially rural-hospital construction, law-enforcement expansion, and a 25% in-state production target - need cost estimates and a clear explanation of what a governor can accomplish without new legislation. Skardon brings policy and organizational experience but has less independently verifiable public-management experience.

## Secretary of State

### Mark Hammond - Republican

Hammond has served as secretary of state since 2003 and was reelected in 2006, 2010, 2014, 2018, and 2022. His official biography documents prior work as a criminal investigator and Spartanburg County clerk of court. The office handles business filings, charities, professional fundraisers, elections-related duties, and public records; see its official services page.

**Critical assessment:** Hammond has the strongest institutional experience. That is useful for continuity but does not automatically demonstrate effectiveness. The relevant questions are processing times, enforcement outcomes, cybersecurity, charity oversight, complaint resolution, and whether the office has become too resistant to modernization after two decades under one leader.

<!-- PDF page 3 -->

### Jason Belton - Democratic

Belton's campaign identifies him as a labor leader, small-business owner, former South Carolina AFL-CIO operations director, and vice president of the Greater Columbia Central Labor Council. His platform emphasizes stronger enforcement against deceptive fundraising, more local access to secretary-of-state services, and expanding the secretary's statutory role, including chairing the Election Commission. See Belton's campaign.

**Critical assessment:** Belton's labor and operational background is relevant to constituent service, and his proposals are more specific than a generic reform message. However, chairing the Election Commission would require confirming the secretary's legal authority and may raise independence questions. His claim that expanded local services could be provided "at no additional cost" needs a staffing, technology, and budget explanation.

## State Treasurer

### Curtis Loftis - Republican

Loftis has served as treasurer since 2011. The treasurer's office says it manages or safeguards more than $76 billion and administers programs including unclaimed property, Future Scholar, and Palmetto ABLE. Those figures and accomplishments are official-office claims documented in his official biography.

Loftis also has the most serious documented controversy among the statewide candidates:

- A state forensic review found no missing or stolen $1.8 billion and no actual surplus of that size, but it did identify accounting and reporting failures during a state-system transition. See the governor's statement.
- The South Carolina Senate adopted a resolution by a 33-8 vote calling for Loftis's removal. The House did not take up the resolution, so he remained in office. See the Senate journal and Associated Press account.
- A 2026 inspector-general investigation, reported by the South Carolina Daily Gazette, concluded that the treasurer's office failed to recognize that the Election Commission could not make payments on a $30.8 million voting-machine loan. The report described a 213.84% compounding late fee and potential exposure to millions of dollars in avoidable damages. Loftis disputed responsibility.

**Critical assessment:** Loftis has substantial experience and no evidence here suggests that he stole money. But the accounting and loan-management issues are directly relevant to the treasurer's core duties. The key questions are whether the office had adequate internal controls, whether warnings were escalated, how responsibility was assigned, and what reforms were implemented.

### Vincent Coe - Democratic

Coe's campaign describes him as a finance professional with a degree from the University of South Carolina, a former FDIC bank examiner, and an executive responsible for fair-lending and Community Reinvestment Act compliance. His biography and platform emphasize monthly public reporting of accounts, investments, and fees; support for community banks; and more transparent investment management.

**Critical assessment:** Coe's banking-regulation background is highly relevant to risk control and consumer protection. He has no comparable elected-office record, however. His transparency proposals are concrete in concept but need implementation details: Which reports would be published, how quickly, under what privacy rules, and at what technology cost? His campaign's criticisms of Loftis should be tested against official reviews rather than accepted solely because they are politically useful.

## Attorney general

### David Stumbo - Republican

Stumbo is the solicitor for South Carolina's Eighth Judicial Circuit and campaigns as a conservative prosecutor. His priorities emphasize public safety, parental rights, religious liberty, law enforcement, and opposition to what he describes as progressive criminal-justice policies. See his campaign site.

**Critical assessment:** prosecutorial experience is relevant to criminal enforcement, but the attorney general also manages civil litigation, consumer protection, antitrust, environmental enforcement, constitutional questions, and legal advice to state agencies. Stumbo's public messaging is strongest on criminal justice and social issues; voters should press him for detailed plans on consumer fraud, corporate misconduct, agency independence, and civil-rights enforcement.

<!-- PDF page 4 -->

### Richard Hricik - Democratic

Hricik is an attorney and former congressional aide who previously ran for the state Senate. His campaign emphasizes rule of law, public-interest litigation, corporate accountability, environmental enforcement, and protecting consumers from large corporations. He also says he worked as an accountant before becoming a lawyer. See the campaign site and policy proposals.

**Critical assessment:** Hricik's accounting background could be useful in financial-fraud and consumer cases, and his campaign provides more detail than a purely slogan-based platform. He lacks an elected or statewide executive record, so there is less evidence about management, litigation judgment, and handling law-enforcement agencies. Voters should ask which cases he would prioritize, how he would control outside litigation costs, and how he would preserve independence from national partisan organizations.

## Comptroller general

The office is South Carolina's chief accounting and fiscal-control office: it supervises state spending, maintains the state's books, processes payments, and advises agencies on accounting. See the official comptroller-general description.

### Mike Burkhold - Republican

Burkhold's campaign centers on the claim that the state's accounting system was off by approximately $3.5 billion and says he has spent 15 years explaining financial numbers to the public. His platform emphasizes transparency, accountability, and tax reform. See the campaign site and candidate biography.

The $3.5 billion figure refers to the prior comptroller's accounting error, not to Burkhold personally. The underlying error is documented in a South Carolina Senate journal.

**Critical assessment:** Burkhold is running directly on a real and important institutional failure, but his campaign rhetoric needs to be translated into an accounting-control plan. I found less evidence of his experience managing a large public accounting office than of his public criticism of one. The decisive questions are qualifications, proposed audit controls, software governance, independent review, and how he would prevent another error from persisting for years.

### Tiffany Boozer - Democratic

Boozer's campaign identifies her as a CPA and the current Quality Assurance Manager in the Comptroller General's Office. Her prior work includes internal-audit and quality-assurance roles at the South Carolina Department of Health and Environmental Control and the Department of Administration. See her campaign biography.

**Critical assessment:** Boozer has unusually direct experience with internal controls and state accounting systems. That is a strength, but it also creates a legitimate independence question: What was her role in the office's existing control environment, what did she know about prior problems, and how would she distinguish herself from the current administration? Those questions should be answered through documented responsibilities, not insinuation.

## State superintendent of education

### Baba Amin Ojuok - United Citizens

Ojuok's campaign describes him as an educator and founder or superintendent of Uhuru Academy Education Solutions. His agenda emphasizes culturally responsive instruction, early literacy, teacher support, mental health, technology, career readiness, and district accountability. See his campaign site and the United Citizens candidate page.

**Critical assessment:** the platform covers many important education issues, but limited independent verification was located for his institutional role, credentials, or measurable education outcomes. The gap is especially important because the superintendent oversees a large state agency and must work with school districts, legislators, and federal programs. He should publish credentials, prior school-performance data, and a costed first-year plan.

<!-- PDF page 5 -->

### Ellen Weaver - Republican

Weaver is the incumbent superintendent, elected in 2022. Her official biography says she previously worked in education-policy advocacy, led the Palmetto Promise Institute, served on the Education Oversight Committee, and holds degrees from Bob Jones University. Her stated priorities include foundational literacy, teacher recruitment and retention, and parent and business involvement.

**Critical assessment:** Weaver has the advantage of an actual statewide administrative record. Voters should evaluate outcomes rather than labels: reading performance, teacher vacancies, district finances, discipline, curriculum decisions, special-education compliance, and whether reforms were implemented consistently. Policy advocacy experience can be valuable, but it is not the same as running a complex agency.

### Sylvia Wright - Democratic

Wright's campaign describes more than 20 years of work as a teacher, instructional coach, district coordinator, and education administrator. It lists degrees in middle-grade education, history, divergent learning, and educational leadership, with an Ed.D. in progress. Her priorities include teacher support, safe and rigorous schools, efficient spending, family engagement, and opposition to book bans and vouchers. See her campaign biography.

**Critical assessment:** Wright has the strongest classroom and district-level practitioner profile among the superintendent candidates. She does not have a statewide elected record, and many of her proposals remain broad. The important follow-ups are how she would measure literacy, fund teacher retention, handle school choice, and balance state standards with district autonomy.

## Commissioner of agriculture

This is not simply a symbolic farming office. The department regulates food safety, weights and measures, fuel pumps, animal feed, agricultural marketing, hemp, licenses, and inspections. See the South Carolina Department of Agriculture statutory authority and agency structure.

### Chris Nelums - United Citizens

Nelums is listed by the United Citizens Party as its agriculture candidate. His campaign identity and public materials suggest particular interest in hemp and alternative agricultural policy. See the party candidate page.

**Critical assessment:** little independent documentation was located regarding management experience, agricultural operations, or detailed plans for food safety, inspections, farm support, land loss, and water policy. That does not establish a lack of qualifications; it means the candidate needs to provide more primary evidence and a clearer operational agenda.

### Cody Simpson - Republican

Simpson's campaign describes him as a fifth-generation South Carolina farmer, former USDA Farm Service Agency state executive director, former adviser to Governor McMaster, and participant in forestry and agricultural organizations. See his campaign biography. His federal biography provides additional confirmation of his USDA role in this USDA biography.

**Critical assessment:** Simpson has some of the most directly relevant agricultural and administrative experience in this race. The main question is whether his agenda extends beyond endorsements and agricultural promotion into difficult regulatory questions: food-safety enforcement, water usage, land preservation, beginning farmers, labor, hemp regulation, and conflicts between large agribusiness and small producers.

### Michael Sullens - Libertarian

Sullens is on the official candidate list, but very little reliable public information was located about his professional background or detailed agriculture platform beyond his Libertarian nomination.

**Critical assessment:** this is a significant information gap. Party affiliation alone does not establish positions on food safety, land use, farm subsidies, water, hemp, or consumer protection. He should publish a biography and specific statutory agenda before voters can evaluate him fairly.

<!-- PDF page 6 -->

### DeShawn Blanding - Democratic

Blanding's campaign describes him as a fourth-generation farmer, cooperative-business advocate, former National FFA officer, agricultural-policy specialist, and former congressional agricultural-policy staffer. His platform emphasizes regional food infrastructure, food hubs, land protection, local food systems, and support for historically underserved producers. See his biography and issues page.

**Critical assessment:** Blanding has substantial subject-matter and policy experience, and his "Rooted Forward" platform is more developed than many agriculture campaigns. Several proposals require appropriations, federal coordination, and partnerships with local governments. Statistics used in campaign materials about land loss and food insecurity should be independently sourced before treating them as established facts.

## United States Senate

### Darline Graham - Republican

Graham was appointed to the Senate after the death of Lindsey Graham and later won the Republican nomination for the full term. Her official biography documents roughly 30 years in vocational rehabilitation and disability employment, including leadership at the South Carolina Commission for the Blind. See the governor's appointment announcement and U.S. House biography.

The Associated Press described her as a political newcomer with limited national-policy experience during the compressed special-election campaign. See AP background. Her campaign emphasizes service, South Carolina roots, and support from President Trump. See her campaign site.

**Critical assessment:** Graham brings substantial agency and disability-services experience, but that is different from legislative experience on defense, trade, taxation, entitlement programs, foreign policy, and federal regulation. Her family connection and presidential endorsement are political facts, not evidence of policy competence. The most important unresolved issue is what she would actually do in the Senate on major national questions.

### Kasie Whitener - Libertarian

Whitener's campaign identifies her as an educator, author, community leader, and radio personality. Her platform emphasizes balanced-budget requirements, debt reduction, government oversight, reform of Social Security and Medicare, and reduced protectionism. See the campaign home page and campaign-finance page.

A positive feature is that her campaign says it publishes a general ledger and donor/spending information. Critical questions remain about the tradeoffs of a balanced-budget amendment, entitlement changes, foreign policy, health care, and how a single senator would implement those goals. She has no legislative record, and limited campaign resources should not be confused with either policy quality or policy weakness.

### Annie Andrews - Democratic

Andrews is a pediatrician and professor who has spent much of her career treating children. Her campaign platform focuses on health-care access, a public option, affordability, schools and childcare, reproductive rights, gun violence, immigration, technology, infrastructure, and ending "endless wars." See her platform and policy announcement.

**Critical assessment:** medical expertise is directly relevant to health, children, and public-health policy, but it does not automatically translate into legislative or foreign-policy expertise. The platform is broad and values-driven; it needs fiscal details, especially for the public option, childcare, housing, and infrastructure. She has no comparable elected voting record.

### Mark Hackett - Constitution

Hackett is the Constitution Party candidate. No substantial campaign website or detailed primary-source platform comparable to the other Senate candidates was located.

**Critical assessment:** this is the largest information gap in the Senate race. It would be irresponsible to infer his positions solely from the Constitution Party label. Voters should seek direct answers on taxes, abortion, immigration, federal authority, military policy, foreign affairs, health care, and constitutional interpretation.

### William Timmons - Republican

Timmons entered Congress in 2019. His official House biography identifies him as a former prosecutor, small-business owner, state senator, and South Carolina Air National Guard JAG officer. He serves on Financial Services and Oversight-related committees.

**Critical assessment:** Timmons has an actual congressional voting and committee record that should be examined directly rather than relying on campaign advertising. ### Courtney McClain - Democratic

McClain's campaign emphasizes affordable housing, health care, cost of living, managing rapid growth, and Upstate economic development. The campaign describes her as a communications professional and Spartanburg-area candidate. See the campaign site.

She has no prior elected record, so voters must evaluate the quality of her proposals and ability to build coalitions rather than past votes.

## South Carolina House, District 17

### Mike Burns - Republican

Burns has represented District 17 since a 2013 special election. Official legislative records document his incumbency. See the 2013 special-election record and legislative reference book.

**Critical assessment:** Burns has the advantage of a long legislative record, but no substantial current campaign platform was located. The right way to evaluate him is through roll-call votes, committee work, constituent service, and specific positions on growth, roads, schools, taxes, and development. An interest-group scorecard can be informative but should not be treated as an objective grade.

### Rich Nicoloff - Democratic

Nicoloff's candidacy and filing are confirmed by the South Carolina election system. Public campaign materials emphasize public education, constitutional rights, and sustainable or regionally responsible growth. See this candidate profile.

**Critical assessment:** Nicoloff appears to bring local small-business and community experience but no prior legislative record. His themes are relevant to the district but need translation into bills, funding sources, and measurable goals. The district's partisan structure affects electability, not the intrinsic quality of his policy ideas.

## Solicitor, Circuit 13

### Cindy Crick - Republican

Crick is the current 13th Circuit solicitor for Greenville and Pickens counties, appointed by Governor McMaster in 2025. Her biography includes a law degree, work with a domestic-violence shelter, assistant-solicitor experience, nearly a decade as chief of staff to Congressman Trey Gowdy, and private legal practice. See her campaign biography and the official circuit-solicitor directory.

**Critical assessment:** she has relevant prosecutorial, legal, and government-management experience. Because she was appointed rather than elected to the current term, voters should focus on charging and plea data, diversion programs, treatment of domestic-violence cases, victim services, disclosure practices, conviction integrity, and prosecutorial transparency. Campaign descriptions such as "tough but fair" are not substitutes for those metrics.

## Probate judge

### Chadwicke L. Groover - Republican

Groover was elected Greenville probate judge in 2022. The South Carolina Courts biography documents more than 14 years of private practice in elder law, probate, trusts, guardianships, and Medicaid planning, as well as prior federal prosecution and Senate Judiciary Committee work.

**Critical assessment:** Groover's legal background is unusually well matched to probate court. The important evaluation questions are case timeliness, guardianship oversight, access for families without lawyers, treatment of vulnerable adults, and administrative performance. The ballot lists no other named candidate, but that should not eliminate scrutiny.

## County auditor

### Scott Case - Republican

Case's official county biography identifies him as a CPA with more than 30 years of accounting and tax experience, a former Greenville County Council member, and auditor since 2007. The county page credits him with work involving county finances, road planning, and economic-development organizations.

**Critical assessment:** Case has strong accounting and local-government experience. Most accomplishment claims come from the office itself, so voters should independently inspect audit findings, property-tax administration, assessment appeals, internal controls, and relationships with economic-development groups. Long tenure is evidence of experience, not automatic proof of current effectiveness.

## County treasurer

### Allen Hodges - Republican

Hodges was elected in 2022 and took office in 2023. The county biography identifies him as a CPA with more than 40 years of finance, accounting, controller, and chief-financial-officer experience.

**Critical assessment:** Hodges has strong technical credentials but a relatively short public-office record. Little detailed campaign-platform material was located. The relevant questions are investment policy, tax collection and delinquency, internal controls, customer service, cybersecurity, and how quickly the office resolves billing errors. No other named candidate appears on the ballot.

## County Council, District 17

### Joe Dill - Republican

Dill previously served on Greenville County Council for roughly two decades and has also served with the county election commission and Appalachian Council of Governments. Reporting describes growth, roads, overloaded public services, farmland preservation, and subdivision pressure as central concerns. See candidate reporting.

**Critical assessment:** Dill brings deep institutional knowledge and a clearly defined growth-management concern. The risk of relying only on biography is that long service can obscure specific decisions. Voters should examine his zoning, road, development, and land-preservation votes, including how he would balance property rights, housing supply, farmland protection, school capacity, traffic, and county infrastructure.

## Soil and Water Conservation District Commission

This is a nonpartisan, specialized conservation board - not a general county legislative office. South Carolina conservation districts address erosion, flooding, water use, soil conservation, demonstration projects, and cooperation with landowners and government agencies. See the SCDNR conservation-district handbook.

The ballot allows two selections.

### Christopher Hall - Nonpartisan

Hall is listed by Greenville County as an elected soil-and-water commissioner. See the county directory.

Little campaign-specific material was located. The proper evaluation questions are attendance, watershed and stormwater work, conservation grants, technical assistance to landowners, development review, and conflicts involving environmental or development organizations.

### Mike Stewart - Nonpartisan

Stewart is also a current elected commissioner and is listed as chair of the Greenville district. See the county directory.

Stewart's current chair role provides some evidence of organizational involvement, but little detailed public campaign material was located. Voters should examine meeting minutes, projects completed, grants obtained, public education, and whether the district has addressed flooding and erosion in a measurable way.

## Most important information gaps

The candidates for whom the least independently verifiable information was located were:

- Candace Brewer
- Arnold E. Karr
- Michael Sullens
- Mark Hackett
- Chris Nelums
- Baba Amin Ojuok, beyond his campaign's self-description

That is not a negative finding. It means those candidates have made it harder for voters to evaluate qualifications, policy details, funding, and past performance.

The most consequential documented controversies are in the treasurer and comptroller contests, because those offices are directly responsible for financial controls. The strongest documented incumbent experience appears in the secretary of state, treasurer, comptroller, superintendent, congressional, solicitor, probate, auditor, and county-treasurer races - but experience should be weighed against actual performance, transparency, and measurable outcomes.

## High-value questions for every campaign

Before relying on any candidate's promises, ask for:

1. A first-year budget with revenue sources and implementation costs.
2. A list of proposals requiring legislation, federal approval, or constitutional changes.
3. Specific measurable outcomes and deadlines.
4. Campaign-finance, contractor, employer, and family-conflict disclosures.
5. A record of votes, audits, case outcomes, or administrative metrics where applicable.

Campaign websites are useful for learning what candidates say about themselves. They are not independent proof that the claims are true.
